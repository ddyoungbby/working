Is the edge robust, or one lucky setting?

Hold-out net dollars for all 18 distinct swing configurations. 11 of 18 made money on data none of them were chosen on; the deployed setting is outlined.

The deployed swing plan

Setup: 20 EMA pullback, daily chartUptrend (price over a rising 20 EMA, 20 over 50), a controlled 1.5-15% pullback, quality flags, and a market filter that blocks bearish tapes.
Entry: buy-stop at the pullback highThe order only fills if price trades through the trigger. If the stock gaps more than 0.75R past it at the open, skip it.
Exit: stop, 2R target, or 10 sessionsResting stop under the pullback low, resting limit at 2R, otherwise sell at the close of the 10th session.
Size: $100 risk, max 3 positionsShares = $100 ÷ (entry − stop), capped at one third of the account per position. One new order per day at most.

Before you trade it

  • It roughly matched the index, it did not beat it. Over the hold-out window SPY rose 12.7%; the swing lane made +$602 on $5,000 (12.0%) with a deeper worst drawdown ($781, about 16%). Its value is a rules-based edge that is independent of simply owning SPY, not superior returns.
  • Expect long losing streaks. At a 38% win rate, 5-8 losers in a row are normal. The plan only works if every trade is taken at the planned size.
  • The universe leans toward survivors. The 81 tickers were picked in 2026, so they skew toward names that are popular now. The 2026 hold-out is the fairer read, and it is weaker than training.
  • Costs are modeled, not measured. One basis point of slippage per side, no commissions on stock. The option alternative in the daily report is not backtested.
  • The forward record is the real test. The daily report tracks every position from Oct 16, 2026 onward in the same rules.
